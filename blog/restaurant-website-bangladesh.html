What should a restaurant website in Bangladesh have?

By Rayan · 26 November 2026 · 4 min read

Most customers in Bangladesh find a restaurant on their phone first — they search, look at the menu and prices, and then decide whether to order or visit. A Facebook page alone is not enough: posts get buried, the menu is hard to find, and orders come in as messy inbox messages. A proper restaurant website fixes all of that. Here is what it should have.

1. A searchable menu with photos and prices

The menu is the reason people visit. It should be searchable, grouped by category (starters, mains, desserts, drinks), with a photo and a clear price for every dish. Customers decide in seconds — if they cannot find the price, they leave. My restaurant demo, Spice House, shows exactly this: search any dish, filter by category, and see the price right away.

2. Online ordering — without middleman commission

When customers order through an app, the restaurant pays commission on every order. On your own website, orders come straight to you — by WhatsApp or through a simple checkout — with no commission. A cart, cash on delivery, and bKash payment options cover how most Bangladeshi customers actually pay.

3. Table booking

For dine-in restaurants, a table booking form saves phone calls: the customer picks a date, time and party size, and the booking lands in your inbox or WhatsApp. It should take under a minute to complete, on a phone.

4. Google Maps, opening hours and contact

Customers need to find you. A Google Map with your location, correct opening hours, and a click-to-call button should be on every page — not hidden on a contact page nobody opens. This also helps your restaurant show up when people nearby search on Google Maps.

5. Photos and reviews

Real photos of your food and your place sell more than any description. Add customer reviews from Google — people trust other customers more than they trust the restaurant itself.

6. Fast on mobile data

A heavy website with big uncompressed photos takes 8 seconds to load on mobile data — and the customer is gone. A restaurant site must be light and fast, designed for the phone first.
